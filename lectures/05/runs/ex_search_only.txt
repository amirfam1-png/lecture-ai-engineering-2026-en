LoRA and QLoRA: adapting a large model on one GPU
Freeze the original weights and train only a small change, or delta, with LoRA. QLoRA also compresses the original weights to 4 bits.
Sources: E. J. Hu et al., “LoRA”, ICLR 2022; T. Dettmers et al., “QLoRA”, NeurIPS 2023. The exercise uses transformers, peft, bitsandbytes and TRL.
LoRA, Hu et al., Fig. 1: train only A and B.
Blue — original weights W: frozen, not trained. QLoRA stores them at 4 bits, a coarser numerical representation, reducing memory to about one quarter. About 4 GB for 7B.
Orange — small matrices A and B: only these are trained. The product of two thin matrices represents the change, with low rank.
Analogy: leave the main model unchanged and add thin sticky notes. The trainable portion can be less than 1% of the total.
The QLoRA paper fine-tuned a 65-billion-parameter model on one 48 GB GPU.
• Even a Colab GPU, for example 16 GB, can adapt a model with billions of parameters. This is today's exercise.
• Data preparation, evaluation and operation still require separate effort.

4/4: when and how to adapt the selected model
PEFT: train only part of a model, not the whole model.
FT that updates every parameter is expensive. Methods that train only a small number of parameters, or PEFT, have become mainstream.
Sources: original papers by Houlsby et al. (2019), Li and Liang (2021), Lester et al. (2021), and Hu et al. (2022). Available through the Hugging Face PEFT library.
What is trained, Dettmers et al., Fig. 1:
Full Fine-Tuning: update all parameters; large memory and storage requirements.
LoRA: freeze the base model and update only a small added component.
QLoRA: also compress the base model to 4 bits to save memory.
Main PEFT methods:
Adapters: insert small layers between existing layers and train only those. Houlsby et al., 2019.
Prefix/prompt tuning: prepend trainable virtual tokens to the input. Li and Liang, 2021; Lester et al., 2021.
LoRA: represent and learn weight changes as the product of two small, thin matrices, a low-rank representation. Hu et al., 2022. Currently the most widely used approach.
QLoRA: LoRA plus compression of original weights to 4 bits for even lower memory use. Dettmers et al., 2023; next slide.

Title: Evidence and debate: RAG often works better for adding knowledge
Page: Lecture 2 · translated PDF p.79

4/4: when and how to adapt the selected model
Evidence and debate: RAG often works better for adding knowledge.
This does not mean FT can add no knowledge at all. Treat it as a general rule while understanding the exceptions.
Sources: Ovadia et al., EMNLP 2024; Gekhman et al., EMNLP 2024; Biderman et al., TMLR 2024; Zhou et al., LIMA, NeurIPS 2023.
Research findings underlying the general rule:
Ovadia et al. (2024): on knowledge tasks, RAG consistently outperformed FT directly on documents.
Gekhman et al. (2024): examples containing new knowledge are learned slowly; learning more of them tends to increase errors, or hallucinations.
Biderman et al. (2024): LoRA learns less and forgets less than full FT. It suits small adjustments.
Exceptions and differing findings:
• Continued pre-training, or CPT, on large amounts of text can add language and domain knowledge, as in Japanese Swallow models.
• Training on varied paraphrases of the same fact can make knowledge easier to learn through FT.
• About 1,000 high-quality examples can substantially change behavior, as in LIMA.
• Transferring a large model's behavior to a small one, or distillation, is also used to reduce cost and latency.

Exercise
Exercise steps and deliverables — tentative
Times are approximate. It is important to use the same questions, temperature and number of runs before and after.
1. Preparation, 10 minutes: fix problems from the preparatory homework and check that the API works.
2. Measure before FT, 10 minutes: use 30 evaluation questions × 3 runs to measure format compliance. Also save answers to 10 ordinary questions.
3. Train with QLoRA, 20 minutes: train on 200 examples, taking roughly ten to twenty minutes on a Colab GPU.
4. Measure after FT, 10 minutes: replace the model and use the same questions, temperature and number of runs as in step 2.
5. Control — knowledge questions, 5 minutes: the examples include no facts, so the results should not change. If they do, suspect an evaluation or training error.
Deliverables:
• A before/after comparison table: format compliance and answers to ordinary questions.
• Two examples of questions that were not fixed.
• A three-line discussion of what improved, what did not, and why.
Use 30 evaluation questions that do not overlap with training data. Small differences may be within the noise, so report variation across the three runs too.
