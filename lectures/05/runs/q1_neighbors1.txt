According to Lecture 02 (pp. 51–52):

* **What LoRA freezes:** The original weights of the base model ($W$), which are kept frozen and not trained.
* **What LoRA trains:** Only small added components—specifically, two thin matrices (referred to as $A$ and $B$) that represent the low-rank change or delta.
* **What QLoRA adds:** In addition to using LoRA, QLoRA **compresses the original weights down to 4 bits** (a coarser numerical representation), which further reduces memory requirements to about one-quarter (around 4 GB for a 7B model) while keeping the original weights frozen.
