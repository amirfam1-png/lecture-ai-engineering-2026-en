In Lecture 02 (specifically Section 4, pages 51–52), LoRA and QLoRA are explained as Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods:

* **What LoRA freezes:** The original model weights ($W$), keeping them completely frozen and untrained (visualized as blue).
* **What LoRA trains:** Only a small, low-rank change (or delta) represented by thin trainable matrices ($A$ and $B$, visualized as orange), which typically make up less than 1% of the total parameters.
* **What QLoRA adds:** It **quantizes/compresses the original frozen weights down to 4-bit precision** (using 4-bit NormalFloat), which drastically reduces the memory required to store the base model (e.g., to about one-quarter of the memory, or roughly 4 GB for a 7-billion parameter model) while keeping the same training setup for the LoRA adapters. This allows large models with billions of parameters to be fine-tuned on modest or single GPUs (like a 16 GB Colab GPU or a single 48 GB GPU). 

*Hint for further exploration:* Think about why freezing the original weights and compressing them to 4 bits saves so much GPU memory during backpropagation compared to standard full fine-tuning.
